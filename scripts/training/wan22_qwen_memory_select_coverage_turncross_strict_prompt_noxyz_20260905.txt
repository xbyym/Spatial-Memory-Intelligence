You are selecting coverage-memory references for video generation.

Input:
- Current frame: the final frame before the next chunk is generated.
- Planned camera motion.
- Candidate coverage frames, ordered by option label.
- Each candidate includes:
  - option: the candidate label, such as A, B, or C

Goal:
Select only candidate frames that have sufficient visible overlap and spatial continuity with Current and can reliably support generation of the expected new viewpoint of the next chunk.

Visible overlap and recoverable spatial continuity are mandatory. Motion alignment and complementary viewpoint coverage are secondary benefits and may be considered only after the mandatory continuity requirement is satisfied.

Core principle:
- A candidate is useful only when enough of its visible region and spatial layout can be matched to Current for the generator to align the two views reliably.
- Semantic similarity alone is not visible overlap. Showing the same kind of scene without a clearly corresponding visible region is insufficient.
- A candidate with little or no visually corresponding region must be rejected, even if it shows a large amount of new viewpoint coverage.
- Correct motion direction, broader context, or stronger complementary coverage never compensates for insufficient visible overlap with Current.

Decision priority:
1. Sufficient visible overlap and spatial continuity with Current.
2. A small, recoverable viewpoint displacement from Current.
3. Alignment with the planned camera motion and support for the expected new viewpoint.
4. Additional complementary coverage.

A higher-priority requirement always overrides a lower-priority benefit.

Important:
- Judge each candidate independently from the actual images and spatial relationship.
- Multiple candidates may be useful at the same time.
- Do not assume that a candidate is suitable merely because of its option label or motion direction.
- Progressive nearby candidates are not redundant when they form a gradual, recoverable viewpoint transition.

For each candidate, determine:
1. Whether Candidate has enough visible overlap and spatial correspondence with Current.
2. Whether Candidate's viewpoint displacement is small enough to be recovered reliably by the generator.
3. Whether Candidate helps cover the expected new viewpoint after the planned camera motion.
4. Whether Candidate is acceptable as a coverage reference.

Motion alignment:
Classify every candidate into exactly one of these three groups:
- supports: aligned with the planned camera motion or helpful for the expected new viewpoint.
- neutral: neither clearly supports nor clearly opposes the planned motion, but may still provide useful continuity.
- opposes: primarily aligned with the opposite direction of an important motion component.

The three motion groups must be mutually exclusive, and together they must contain every displayed candidate exactly once.

Motion alignment and acceptability are separate judgments except for exact turn_left+move_right and turn_right+move_left. For either of these two turn-cross motions, a candidate classified as opposes must not appear in acceptable or selected. A strong continuity anchor that does not clearly oppose the primary turn should be classified as neutral instead. Outside these two exact motions, motion alignment alone does not make a candidate acceptable or unacceptable.

Use the candidate's position relative to Current as guidance, but also consider the actual visual-spatial and visual-semantic context.

Overlap and recoverability guidance:
- A candidate is acceptable only when it has sufficient shared visible region and spatial structure for reliable alignment with Current.
- If only a very small boundary region overlaps, or no stable visual-spatial correspondence can be established, the candidate is not acceptable.
- A large viewpoint jump is unacceptable when the shared visible region is too small for reliable recovery.
- When overlap or recoverability is uncertain, prefer the closer and more continuous candidate.
- Similar nearby candidates may both be useful when they provide a gradual transition. Do not reject them merely for being similar.
- Do not replace a closer, sufficiently aligned candidate with a farther candidate only because the farther candidate provides broader coverage.

Motion guidance:
- Base each decision jointly on visible overlap, spatial continuity, recoverable displacement, motion alignment, and visual-semantic alignment.
- For static or very small motion, prioritize the candidates with the strongest visible overlap and smallest viewpoint change.
- For compound motion, a candidate must remain spatially recoverable while supporting the important motion components.
- For exact turn_left+move_right, prioritize recoverable left-rotation support; treat move_right translation as secondary. Do not reinterpret it as backward widening or prefer bilateral coverage over the left-turn direction.
- For exact turn_right+move_left, prioritize recoverable right-rotation support; treat move_left translation as secondary. Do not reinterpret it as backward widening or prefer bilateral coverage over the right-turn direction.
- For these two exact turn-cross motions, acceptable and selected must not contain any candidate classified as opposes.

Conflict guidance:
- Be conservative when assigning a candidate to opposes.
- Opposing left/right directions may conflict.
- Opposing up/down directions may conflict.
- Forward/backward directions are not automatically conflicting.
- For compound motion, assign opposes only when the candidate clearly conflicts with an important motion component.
- If the direction is uncertain, treat it as neutral rather than opposing.
- Regardless of motion alignment, insufficient visible overlap always makes a candidate unacceptable and unselected.

Selection rules:
- First judge every candidate's visible overlap and recoverable spatial continuity with Current.
- Any candidate that fails this mandatory requirement must not appear in acceptable or selected.
- List every candidate that satisfies all mandatory requirements in acceptable, subject to the strict turn-cross conflict rule above.
- selected must be a subset of acceptable.
- The required selected count is exactly min(3, the number of acceptable candidates).
- If acceptable is [], selected must be [].
- If acceptable contains 1, 2, or 3 candidates, selected must contain all of them.
- If acceptable contains 4 or more candidates, selected must contain exactly the best 3 candidates.
- acceptable must never be non-empty while selected is empty.
- Among acceptable candidates, prefer the closest candidates in supports, followed by strong continuity anchors in neutral.
- selected should include at least one strong continuity anchor whenever an acceptable anchor exists.
- Every selected candidate must have sufficient overlap and recoverability. When more than 3 candidates are acceptable, use motion support, continuity, and recoverable displacement to choose the best 3.
- Progressive nearby candidates may both be selected when they form a gradual transition; do not skip a closer candidate merely to obtain broader complementary coverage.
- Never select a more displaced candidate only for broader coverage when a closer aligned candidate provides adequate support.
- If every candidate has insufficient overlap, excessive displacement, an unrecoverable viewpoint change, or only conflicts with the primary turn direction, both acceptable and selected must be [].

Output fields:
- motion: classify every displayed candidate exactly once under supports, neutral, or opposes.
- acceptable: every candidate that satisfies the mandatory visible-overlap, recoverability, and strict turn-cross conflict requirements.
- selected: the final subset of acceptable candidates, with exactly min(3, len(acceptable)) candidates.
- Use only candidate labels that are actually displayed. If only A and B are displayed, the three motion lists together must contain exactly A and B, not C-G.

Do not describe image content, objects, locations, scene types, or categories.
Output strict JSON only.

Output format:
{
  "motion": {
    "supports": ["A", "B"],
    "neutral": ["C", "D"],
    "opposes": ["E", "F", "G"]
  },
  "acceptable": ["A", "B", "C"],
